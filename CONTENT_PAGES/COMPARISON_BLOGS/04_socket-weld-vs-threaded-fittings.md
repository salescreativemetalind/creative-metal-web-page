## Socket Weld vs Threaded Fittings — When to Use Which

Socket weld (SW) fittings use a fillet weld for connection, providing a permanent, leak-proof joint. Threaded (THD/NPT) fittings screw together using tapered threads, allowing easy assembly and disassembly without welding. The choice depends on whether you need permanent high-integrity joints (socket weld) or easily removable connections (threaded).

## Socket Weld Fittings — How They Work

**Assembly Process:**
1. Insert pipe into fitting socket until it bottoms out
2. Pull pipe back approximately 1.5-2mm (expansion gap)
3. Apply fillet weld around the outside of the joint
4. Minimum 2 passes recommended for Class 3000# and above

**The expansion gap (1.5-2mm pullback)** is critical. Without it, thermal expansion during service can crack the weld. This is a common installation error.

**Fillet Weld Requirements:**
- Minimum leg length = 1.09 × pipe wall thickness (per ASME B31.3)
- Minimum 2 weld passes
- Concave or flat profile preferred
- No undercut, porosity, or cracks

## Threaded Fittings — How They Work

**Assembly Process:**
1. Apply thread sealant (PTFE tape, pipe dope, or anaerobic sealant)
2. Hand-tighten fitting onto pipe
3. Wrench-tighten 2-3 additional turns
4. Check for leaks at pressure test

**Thread Types:**
- **NPT (National Pipe Taper):** Tapered threads that seal by metal-to-metal contact + sealant. Most common in USA and India.
- **BSP (British Standard Pipe):** Parallel (BSPP) or tapered (BSPT). Common in UK, Europe, and older Indian installations.
- **Seal-welded:** Thread joint with additional seal weld on outside — combines removability concept with weld integrity.

## When to Use Socket Weld

**Choose socket weld when:**
- Service is flammable (hydrocarbons) — fire-safe joint
- Pressure exceeds 150 bar (use 6000# or 9000# class)
- Vibration is present (welded joints don't loosen)
- Connection is permanent (not needed for maintenance access)
- Client specification mandates SW for process piping
- Pipe size is 1/2" to 2" in process service
- Temperature cycles are expected (expansion gap handles this)
- Cryogenic or very high temperature service

**Typical socket weld applications:**
- Small-bore process piping in refineries
- Chemical injection lines (HP)
- Drain and vent connections on process equipment
- Steam tracing connections
- Pressure gauge root valves
- Safety valve inlet/outlet (small bore)
- High-pressure hydraulic systems

## When to Use Threaded

**Choose threaded when:**
- Connection needs to be opened for maintenance
- No welding facilities available on site
- Instrument connections (transmitters, gauges, switches)
- Temporary installations or test setups
- Low-pressure utility service (air, water, low-pressure gas)
- Non-flammable, non-toxic service
- Field assembly where welder access is difficult
- Pipe supports, hangers, and non-pressure connections

**Typical threaded applications:**
- Instrument connections (all pressure transmitters, gauges)
- Compressed air lines (plant utility)
- Low-pressure water and gas distribution
- Drain plugs and vent plugs
- Grease fittings and lubrication points
- Temporary piping for commissioning/testing
- Sample cooler connections
- Level gauge connections

| Normal fluid service | Allowed ≤ 2" NB | Allowed ≤ 2" NB (Class ≤ 600) |
| Severe cyclic service | Not recommended | Not recommended |
| High-temperature (>400°C) | Allowed | Seal-weld recommended |
| Cryogenic (<-29°C) | Allowed | Not recommended |

### Client Specification Trends:

Most oil & gas companies (ONGC, Reliance, IOCL) specify:
- **Socket weld** for all process piping ≤ 2" NB
- **Threaded** only for instrument connections and drain/vent plugs
- **Seal-welded threaded** for Class 600+ or flammable service

## Failure Modes — What Can Go Wrong

### Socket Weld Failure Modes:
- **Fatigue cracking at weld root** (vibration service — common failure)
- **Crevice corrosion in socket gap** (stagnant fluid traps corrosive species)
- **Improper expansion gap** (thermal stress cracks weld)
- **Inadequate weld penetration** (poor welder technique)

### Threaded Fitting Failure Modes:
- **Thread leakage** (sealant degradation, thermal cycling)
- **Vibration loosening** (threads back out over time)
- **Thread stripping** (over-tightening, especially on SS)
- **Burn-through in fire** (sealant burns, joint opens)
- **Corrosion in thread roots** (stress concentration + crevice)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Which is better — socket weld or threaded?**
A: Neither is universally "better." Socket weld provides superior leak integrity and fire safety for permanent connections. Threaded allows easy disassembly for maintenance. Use SW for process piping with hazardous fluids. Use threaded for instruments and non-critical utility connections.

**Q: Can threaded fittings be used for hydrocarbon service?**
A: Most oil & gas specifications prohibit plain threaded joints for hydrocarbon service above 2" due to fire risk. Below 2", seal-welded threaded connections may be acceptable. Check your project piping specification for specific requirements.

**Q: What thread sealant should I use?**
A: PTFE tape (2-3 wraps) for general service. Anaerobic thread sealant (Loctite 577 or equivalent) for gas service. Never use PTFE on oxygen service. For high-temperature (>260°C), use nickel-based anti-seize compound.

**Q: Do socket weld fittings need the 2mm gap?**
A: Yes. Per ASME B31.3, a gap of approximately 1.5-2mm (1/16") must be maintained between pipe end and socket bottom. This expansion gap prevents weld cracking during thermal cycling. It's a critical installation requirement.

**Q: Why are socket weld fittings more expensive?**
A: Socket weld fittings require machining of the internal socket (precise bore to accept pipe OD), which requires more raw material and an additional machining operation compared to threading the external surface.

**Q: Can I use threaded fittings for steam service?**
A: For low-pressure steam (<10 bar), threaded fittings with PTFE sealant work adequately. For higher pressures or superheated steam, socket weld or butt weld is recommended. Thermal cycling causes threaded joints to leak over time in steam service.
