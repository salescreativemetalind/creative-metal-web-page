## Why Copper Nickel for Seawater?

**1. Biofouling Resistance**
Copper-nickel naturally inhibits marine organism attachment (barnacles, mussels, algae). This reduces maintenance and keeps piping clean without anti-fouling chemicals — a unique property no steel alloy offers.

**2. Excellent Seawater Corrosion Resistance**
CuNi alloys form a thin, adherent protective film in seawater that provides excellent long-term corrosion resistance. Typical corrosion rate: 0.025 mm/year (less than 1mm in 40 years).

**3. Erosion-Corrosion Resistance**
At high flow velocities (up to 3.5 m/s for 90/10 and 4.5 m/s for 70/30), CuNi maintains its protective film. This is critical for seawater cooling systems with high-velocity flow.

**4. Good Thermal Conductivity**
Copper-nickel has much higher thermal conductivity (40-50 W/m·K) than stainless steel (15 W/m·K), making it ideal for heat exchangers and condensers.

**5. Proven 50+ Year Track Record**
Ships, offshore platforms, and desalination plants worldwide have used CuNi piping for decades with documented 40-50+ year service life in seawater.

**Q: Why use copper nickel instead of stainless steel for seawater?**
A: Three reasons: (1) Biofouling resistance — CuNi prevents marine growth that SS cannot. (2) Better corrosion in stagnant/slow seawater — SS is prone to pitting in low-flow seawater. (3) No chloride SCC risk — SS can crack in chloride environments, CuNi cannot.

**Q: What is the difference between CuNi 90/10 and 70/30?**
A: 70/30 has 3x more nickel (30% vs 10%), giving it higher strength, better erosion resistance at high flow velocities, and superior corrosion resistance in polluted seawater. It costs 20-25% more. Use 70/30 for high-velocity or aggressive marine conditions.

**Q: Can copper nickel pipe be welded?**
A: Yes. CuNi is readily weldable using GTAW (TIG) or SMAW with matching filler (ERCuNi for 90/10, ERCuNi for 70/30). Back-purge with argon is recommended. No PWHT required. Welding is straightforward compared to titanium or duplex steel.
