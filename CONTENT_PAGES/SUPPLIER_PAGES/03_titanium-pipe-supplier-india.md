## Why Titanium Pipe? Key Advantages

**1. Exceptional Corrosion Resistance**
Titanium forms a stable, self-healing oxide layer (TiO₂) that provides excellent resistance to seawater, chlorides, chlorine, and many acids. It outperforms SS 316L and even some nickel alloys in chloride environments.

**2. Lightweight & High Strength-to-Weight Ratio**
At 4.51 g/cm³ (vs steel at 7.85), titanium offers the highest strength-to-weight ratio of common engineering metals. Critical for aerospace, marine, and mobile equipment.

**3. Biocompatibility**
Titanium is biologically inert — used in medical implants. For pharmaceutical and food processing, it ensures zero contamination.

**4. High Temperature Capability**
Grade 5 titanium maintains strength up to 400°C. Even Grade 2 performs well up to 300°C in oxidizing environments.

**5. Long Service Life**
Titanium equipment in seawater service routinely lasts 40+ years without significant corrosion — reducing total lifecycle cost despite higher initial investment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is titanium pipe price per kg in India?**
A: Titanium Grade 2 seamless pipe costs ₹3,200-4,500/kg. Grade 5 costs ₹4,500-6,500/kg. Prices depend on size, wall thickness, and quantity. Titanium is expensive but offers 40+ year service life with zero maintenance.

**Q: Why is titanium pipe so expensive?**
A: Titanium ore (rutile/ilmenite) is abundant, but the extraction process (Kroll process) is extremely energy-intensive and slow. The metal must be processed in vacuum or inert atmosphere to prevent contamination. Fabrication into pipe requires specialized equipment and skilled operators.

**Q: Which titanium grade is best for seawater?**
A: Grade 2 handles seawater excellently at temperatures up to 70-80°C. For elevated temperatures or crevice conditions, Grade 7 (with palladium addition) or Grade 12 provides extra safety margin. For heat exchanger tubes in seawater, Grade 2 is the industry standard.

**Q: Can titanium pipe be welded?**
A: Yes, but requires absolute shielding. Titanium absorbs oxygen, nitrogen, and hydrogen above 300°C which causes embrittlement. Welding must use full argon shielding (back-purge + trailing shield). Only GTAW (TIG) process is recommended. Filler wire must match base metal grade.

**Q: Do you stock titanium pipe in India?**
A: We stock Titanium Grade 2 seamless pipes in 1/2" to 3" NB as ready stock. Larger sizes, Grade 5, and Grade 7 are available on 4-8 week delivery from international mills (VSMPO, Toho Titanium, Western Superconducting).

**Q: What is the difference between titanium and Inconel pipe?**
A: Titanium is lighter (4.51 vs 8.44 g/cm³), better in chlorides, and cheaper (₹3,200-4,500 vs ₹3,500-5,000/kg). Inconel is better at very high temperatures (above 600°C) and in reducing acids. For seawater/chloride service below 300°C, titanium is preferred.
