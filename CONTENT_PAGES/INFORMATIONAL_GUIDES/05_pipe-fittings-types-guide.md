## Pipe Fittings Types — Complete Guide

Pipe fittings are components used to connect, redirect, reduce, branch, or close piping systems. They come in various types, materials, sizes, and connection methods. This guide covers all standard pipe fitting types used in industrial piping — from basic elbows to specialized fittings — with their specific applications and selection criteria.

## Categories of Pipe Fittings

| Category | Connection Type | Size Range | Standard |
|----------|----------------|-----------|----------|
| Buttweld Fittings | Butt weld (full penetration) | 1/2" to 48" | ASME B16.9 |
| Forged Fittings | Socket weld or Threaded | 1/8" to 4" | ASME B16.11 |
| Compression Fittings | Ferrule compression | 1/8" to 1" (tube OD) | Manufacturer std |
| Grooved Fittings | Mechanical coupling | 1" to 24" | AWWA, FM/UL |
| Flanged Fittings | Bolted flange | All sizes | ASME B16.5 |

## 1. ELBOWS — Direction Change

Elbows redirect piping direction. Available in 90°, 45°, and custom angles.

### 90° Long Radius (LR) Elbow
- **Centerline radius:** 1.5 × Nominal Diameter (e.g., 4" elbow has 6" radius)
- **Use:** Standard direction change in process piping. Most common fitting in any piping system.
- **When to use:** Default choice for all 90° direction changes unless space-constrained.

### 90° Short Radius (SR) Elbow
- **Centerline radius:** 1.0 × Nominal Diameter (e.g., 4" elbow has 4" radius)
- **Use:** Where space is limited and piping layout is tight
- **When to use:** Only when long radius won't fit. Causes higher pressure drop.
- **Caution:** Higher erosion/corrosion at outer wall, more pressure drop than LR.

### 45° Elbow
- **Centerline radius:** 1.5 × Nominal Diameter
- **Use:** Gradual direction change (less turbulence than 90°)
- **When to use:** Pipeline direction changes, where two 45° elbows provide more gradual routing than one 90°.

### 180° Return Bend (U-Bend)
- **Use:** Reverses pipe direction in compact space
- **Where found:** Heat exchanger tubing, condensate return, small-space piping

### Reducing Elbow
- **Function:** Changes direction AND reduces size simultaneously
- **Use:** Eliminates need for elbow + reducer, saves space and one weld joint

## 2. TEES — Branching

Tees create a 90° branch from the main (run) pipe.

### Equal Tee
- **All three openings same size**
- **Use:** Where branch pipe is same size as header/run pipe
- **Example:** 4" × 4" × 4" Tee

### Reducing Tee
- **Branch (outlet) is smaller than run (header)**
- **Use:** Most common in process piping — branch takeoffs are typically smaller than header
- **Example:** 6" × 6" × 4" Tee (6" run with 4" branch)
- **Naming convention:** Run × Run × Branch

### Lateral (Y-piece)
- **Branch at 45° angle** (not 90° like standard tee)
- **Use:** Reduces turbulence and pressure drop at branch. Used in pipelines and high-velocity flow.

### Weldolet / Sockolet / Thredolet (Branch Fittings)
- **Function:** Creates branch connection directly on pipe wall
- **Use:** When branch is much smaller than header (ratio > 2:1)
- **Advantage:** No full tee needed, saves material and welding
- **Types:**
  - Weldolet: Butt weld connection on branch
  - Sockolet: Socket weld connection on branch
  - Thredolet: Threaded connection on branch
  - Elbolet: Branch on an elbow

## 3. REDUCERS — Size Change

Reducers transition between different pipe sizes.

### Concentric Reducer
- **Centerlines aligned** (both pipes share same centerline)
- **Use:** Vertical piping, equipment nozzle connections
- **When to use:** Default choice unless eccentric is specifically needed

### Eccentric Reducer
- **One side flat** (bottom-flat or top-flat)
- **Use:** Horizontal piping where:
  - Bottom-flat (BOP — Bottom of Pipe flat): Prevents air/gas pockets (pump suction)
  - Top-flat (TOP — Top of Pipe flat): Maintains drainage, prevents liquid pockets
- **Critical rule:** At PUMP SUCTION → use eccentric reducer (flat on top) to prevent cavitation from trapped gas.

### Swage (Forged Reducer)
- **Forged from solid bar** (small bore, high pressure)
- **Concentric or Eccentric** available
- **Use:** Small-bore (1/4" to 2") piping where forged fittings are used

## 4. CAPS & CLOSURES

### Pipe Cap (Buttweld)
- **Closes the end of a pipe** with a welded dome/flat end
- **Use:** Pipeline terminations, dead legs, future connections

### Blind Flange
- **Blanks off a flanged connection** (bolted, not welded)
- **Use:** Equipment isolation, blanking unused nozzles, test points
- **Advantage:** Removable without cutting pipe

### Plug (Threaded)
- **Closes a threaded opening** (hex or square head)
- **Use:** Closing drain points, instrument connections, test connections

## 5. COUPLINGS & UNIONS

### Full Coupling (SW or THD)
- **Joins two pipes of same size** end-to-end
- **Use:** Extending pipe runs in small-bore piping
- **Connection:** Socket weld or threaded

### Half Coupling (SW or THD)
- **Creates a branch connection** (one pipe-connection end, one weld-to-pipe end)
- **Use:** Instrument taps, gauge connections, small branch from large pipe
- **Similar to:** Sockolet but cheaper and simpler geometry

### Reducing Coupling
- **Joins two different pipe sizes**
- **Use:** Size transition in small-bore piping (instead of reducer)

### Union
- **Three-piece fitting** allowing disconnection without cutting
- **Components:** Two end pieces + central nut
- **Use:** Where maintenance disconnection is needed (near equipment, across valves)
- **Advantage:** Can be disassembled without disturbing pipe alignment

## 6. NIPPLES & ADAPTERS

### Pipe Nipple
- **Short piece of pipe** with threads on both ends
- **Lengths:** Close (minimal), Short, Long (up to 12")
- **Use:** Connecting two threaded fittings, extending from equipment

### Hex Nipple
- **Nipple with hex (wrench) flat** in the middle
- **Use:** Tight spaces where wrench grip is needed between two fittings

### Hex Bushing
- **Reduces thread size** (male OD thread to female ID thread)
- **Use:** Reducing connection size without a coupling

### Adapter (Male × Female)
- **Changes connection type** (e.g., NPT to BSP, SW to threaded)
- **Use:** Connecting different thread standards or connection types

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What are the most common types of pipe fittings?**
A: The most common are: 90° elbows (direction change), tees (branching), reducers (size change), and caps (closing ends). These four types make up 90% of all fittings in a typical piping system.

**Q: What is the difference between concentric and eccentric reducer?**
A: Concentric has centered alignment (both pipes share same centerline). Eccentric has one flat side. Use concentric for vertical pipe. Use eccentric for horizontal pipe — flat on bottom at pump suction (prevents gas pockets), flat on top for gravity drainage.

**Q: What is a weldolet?**
A: A weldolet is a branch fitting that welds directly onto the run pipe, creating a branch connection without needing a full tee. Used when branch-to-header size ratio exceeds 1:2 (e.g., 2" branch from 8" header).

**Q: What fitting standard should I specify?**
A: For buttweld fittings: ASME B16.9 (dimensions), ASTM A403 (SS material) or A234 (CS material). For forged fittings: ASME B16.11 (dimensions), ASTM A182 (SS/Alloy) or A105 (CS).
