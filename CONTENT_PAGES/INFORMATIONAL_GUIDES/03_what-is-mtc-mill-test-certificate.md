## What is MTC (Mill Test Certificate)? — Buyer's Guide

A Mill Test Certificate (MTC), also called Material Test Certificate or Mill Cert, is an official document issued by the material manufacturer certifying that the supplied product meets the ordered specification. It contains actual chemical composition, mechanical properties, heat treatment details, and test results from the specific batch (heat) of material being supplied. MTC is the most important quality document in industrial piping procurement.

## Why MTC Matters

Every industrial pipe, tube, fitting, flange, or plate you purchase should come with an MTC because:

1. **Proof of quality:** Confirms material actually meets the specification you ordered
2. **Traceability:** Links the physical material to its specific melt/heat in the mill
3. **Verification:** Allows independent verification of chemical and mechanical properties
4. **Compliance:** Required by codes (ASME, IBR, PED) for pressure-retaining components
5. **Safety:** Ensures correct material is installed in critical service
6. **Liability:** Provides legal documentation if material failure occurs
7. **Audit trail:** Third-party inspectors and end-users can verify material origin

## Types of MTC — EN 10204 Standard

The European standard EN 10204 defines types of inspection documents:

| Type | Name | Who Tests | Who Certifies | Independence |
|------|------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| 2.1 | Declaration of Conformity | Manufacturer | Manufacturer | Low |
| 2.2 | Test Report | Manufacturer | Manufacturer | Low |
| 3.1 | Inspection Certificate | Manufacturer | Manufacturer's QA (independent of production) | Medium |
| 3.2 | Inspection Certificate | Manufacturer | Manufacturer + Third-Party Inspector | High |

### EN 10204 Type 3.1 — Most Common

- Tests conducted by manufacturer's laboratory
- Results certified by manufacturer's quality department (independent from production)
- **This is the standard MTC for most industrial pipe purchases**
- Adequate for general process piping, commercial use

### EN 10204 Type 3.2 — Premium/Critical

- Tests conducted by manufacturer's laboratory
- Results verified and countersigned by an independent Third-Party Inspector (TPI)
- TPI agencies: Lloyd's, Bureau Veritas, SGS, TUV, DNV
- **Required for critical service:** offshore, nuclear, some boiler applications
- Costs more due to TPI fees and scheduling

## How to Read an MTC — Section by Section

A typical MTC contains these sections:

### 1. Header Information
| Field | What to Check |
|-------|---------------|
| Manufacturer Name | Must be a recognized, reputable mill |
| Certificate Number | Unique identifier for this document |
| Date of Issue | Should be around manufacturing date |
| Order/PO Reference | Should match your purchase order |
| Customer Name | Should be your company or your supplier |

### 2. Material Identification
| Field | What to Check |
|-------|---------------|
| Specification | Must match ordered spec (e.g., ASTM A312 TP316L) |
| Grade | Must match ordered grade |
| Heat Number | Unique melt identifier — must match marking on pipe |
| Lot/Batch Number | For traceability |
| Size & Dimensions | Must match ordered size |
| Quantity | Should match delivery quantity |

### 3. Chemical Composition
| Field | What to Check |
|-------|---------------|
| Elements listed | All required elements per specification |
| Values | Must fall WITHIN specification limits (min and max) |
| Analysis type | "Heat analysis" (ladle) or "Product analysis" (from pipe) |

**Example — SS 316L Chemical Composition Check:**

| Element | Spec Requirement | Actual (MTC) | Pass/Fail |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|-----------|
| C | 0.030% max | 0.022% | ✅ Pass |
| Cr | 16.0-18.0% | 16.82% | ✅ Pass |
| Ni | 10.0-14.0% | 10.15% | ✅ Pass |
| Mo | 2.0-3.0% | 2.08% | ✅ Pass |
| Mn | 2.0% max | 1.45% | ✅ Pass |
| Si | 1.0% max | 0.38% | ✅ Pass |
| P | 0.045% max | 0.032% | ✅ Pass |
| S | 0.030% max | 0.001% | ✅ Pass |

### 4. Mechanical Properties
| Field | What to Check |
|-------|---------------|
| Tensile Strength | Must meet minimum per spec |
| Yield Strength (0.2%) | Must meet minimum per spec |
| Elongation | Must meet minimum per spec |
| Hardness | Must not exceed maximum per spec |
| Impact Test (if applicable) | Must meet minimum absorbed energy |

### 5. Test & Inspection Results
| Test | What to Verify |
|------|----------------|
| Hydrostatic Test | "Satisfactory" or actual pressure applied |
| Flattening/Bend Test | "Satisfactory" (no cracks) |
| NDT (UT/ECT/RT) | "Acceptable" per specification criteria |
| IGC Test (for SS) | "No attack" per ASTM A262 Practice E |
| Surface Inspection | "Acceptable" — free from defects |

### 6. Heat Treatment
| Field | What to Check |
|-------|---------------|
| Treatment type | Solution annealing (for SS), Normalizing (for CS) |
| Temperature | Within specification range |
| Holding time | Adequate for section size |
| Cooling method | Water quench (SS), Air cool (CS varies) |

## Common MTC Red Flags

Watch for these issues that indicate potential problems:

| Red Flag | What It Means |
|----------|---------------|
| Chemical values at exact spec limits | May indicate false reporting |
| All heats showing identical values | Likely copy-paste fabrication |
| Heat number doesn't match material marking | Wrong MTC or wrong material |
| Missing elements in composition | Incomplete testing |
| No inspector stamp on 3.2 cert | Not genuinely third-party verified |
| Test temperature not specified for Charpy | Results may be meaningless |
| Certificate date long before or after manufacturing | Suspicious timing |
| Grade doesn't match what you ordered | CRITICAL — wrong material supplied |

## What to Do If MTC Shows Non-Conformance

If any value falls outside specification:

1. **Stop — do not install the material**
2. **Inform supplier immediately** with specific non-conformance details
3. **Request re-testing** if you suspect lab error
4. **Seek concession (deviation approval)** from design engineer if value is marginally outside
5. **Reject material** if non-conformance is significant or concession is denied
6. **Document everything** — photos of marking, copy of MTC, correspondence

## MTC vs Other Certificates

| Document | What It Is | When Required |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| MTC (3.1/3.2) | Material test certificate from mill | Every purchase |
| IBR Form III-C | Indian Boiler Regulation certificate | Boiler applications only |
| PED Declaration | European Pressure Equipment compliance | EU/export projects |
| PMI Report | Portable XRF verification of alloy | Project requirement |
| Coating Certificate | Paint/coating thickness and adhesion | Coated pipes |
| Dimensional Report | Actual measured dimensions | Critical tolerance applications |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is MTC in piping?**
A: MTC (Mill Test Certificate) is the official quality document from the material manufacturer showing actual chemical composition, mechanical properties, and test results for the specific batch of material. It proves the material meets the ordered specification.

**Q: What is the difference between MTC 3.1 and 3.2?**
A: Both are issued by the manufacturer. The difference is that 3.2 is additionally countersigned by an independent third-party inspector (Lloyd's, BV, etc.) who witnessed the testing. 3.2 provides higher assurance and is required for critical/offshore applications.

**Q: Is MTC mandatory for all pipe purchases?**
A: For industrial process piping — yes, it should always be demanded. Codes like ASME B31.3 require material identification and traceability. For non-pressure applications (structural, decorative), MTC is good practice but may not be code-mandated.

**Q: Can MTC be faked?**
A: Unfortunately, yes — fake MTCs exist in the market. Verify by: (1) checking heat number on physical material matches MTC, (2) confirming manufacturer is reputable, (3) doing PMI test on material, (4) requesting 3.2 cert with known TPI agency for critical applications.

**Q: Do you provide MTC with all materials?**
A: Yes. Every pipe, fitting, flange, and plate we supply comes with EN 10204 Type 3.1 MTC from the manufacturer. Type 3.2 (with TPI) available on request for critical projects.
