## Why is Inconel Pipe So Expensive?

Inconel 625 costs 10-15x more than SS 304 per kg. Here's why:

**1. Very High Nickel Content (58-72%)**
Nickel costs approximately $16,000-18,000/tonne (₹1,350-1,500/kg) on LME. Inconel 625 contains 58% nickel minimum — that's ₹783-870/kg just in nickel raw material cost alone.

**2. Expensive Alloying Elements**
- Molybdenum (8-10%): ~₹3,500/kg
- Chromium (20-23%): ~₹800/kg
- Niobium (3.15-4.15%): ~₹3,000/kg
Raw material cost alone exceeds ₹1,500-2,000/kg before manufacturing.

**3. Difficult Manufacturing**
Inconel is extremely difficult to hot-work and cold-draw. It work-hardens rapidly, requiring specialized equipment, slower production speeds, and frequent tool replacement.

**4. Limited Global Production**
Only a handful of mills produce Inconel pipe: Special Metals (USA), VDM Metals (Germany), Nippon Yakin (Japan), and a few Indian producers. Limited supply keeps prices high.

**5. Extensive Testing**
Every lot requires: chemical analysis, mechanical testing, corrosion testing, microstructure examination, and often NACE compliance testing. Testing costs add ₹100-200/kg.

## When to Use Inconel 625 (Justifying the Cost)

**Inconel 625 is required when:**
- Temperature exceeds 600°C with corrosive environment
- Exposure to highly oxidizing and reducing environments simultaneously
- Seawater above 40°C (too aggressive for duplex)
- Flue gas containing SO₂ + HCl at high temperature
- Nuclear applications requiring radiation resistance
- Aerospace exhaust and combustion applications

**Cost-effective alternatives to consider:**
- Below 300°C chloride: Use Duplex 2205 (₹650-900/kg) — saves 60%
- Mild acid/alkali below 200°C: Use SS 316L (₹330-480/kg) — saves 85%
- High temp but non-corrosive: Use Inconel 600 (₹2,800-3,800/kg) — saves 20%
- Reducing acids only: Use Monel 400 (₹2,500-3,500/kg) — saves 25%

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is Inconel 625 pipe price per kg in India?**
A: Inconel 625 seamless pipe costs ₹3,500-5,000/kg depending on size and schedule. Smaller sizes (1/2"-1") are most expensive. Prices fluctuate with global nickel LME rates.

**Q: Why is Inconel so much more expensive than stainless steel?**
A: Inconel contains 58-72% nickel (vs 8-14% in SS), plus expensive molybdenum and niobium. Raw materials alone cost ₹1,500-2,000/kg. Manufacturing is difficult and slow. Production volumes are tiny compared to stainless steel.

**Q: Is there a cheaper alternative to Inconel 625?**
A: Depends on your application. For seawater below 40°C: Super Duplex 2507 (₹900-1200/kg). For reducing acids: Monel 400 (₹2,500-3,500/kg). For high temperature without severe corrosion: Inconel 600 (₹2,800-3,800/kg). Consult our engineers for the right selection.

**Q: Do you stock Inconel 625 pipe?**

**Q: What is the difference between Inconel 625 and Inconel 600?**
A: Inconel 625 has molybdenum (8-10%) and niobium (3.15-4.15%) which Inconel 600 lacks. This gives 625 much better corrosion resistance and higher strength. 600 is primarily for high-temperature oxidation resistance. 625 costs 20-30% more than 600.

**Q: Can Inconel 625 be welded?**
A: Yes. Inconel 625 has excellent weldability using GTAW (TIG) or GMAW with matching filler wire ERNiCrMo-3. No PWHT is normally required. It's one of the most weldable nickel alloys available.
